# Knowledge: Creator-Coach Funnel & Conversion Math

*Compiled Aug 2026. Re-check interval noted per section.*

## The mechanic (confirmed by the teardown, consistent with research)

Keyword-in-caption → comment or DM the keyword → automated/manual DM
opens a conversation → conversation becomes a coaching call → call closes
or doesn't. Every one of the five creators in `competitors.md` runs some
version of this. It costs nothing to set up and Instagram's own ranking
rewards DM activity independent of its funnel value (see `platform.md` —
DM sends are a top engagement signal).

## Conversion rate data — read the source-quality warning first

**[unverified — vendor marketing]** The specific numbers found in research
(DM funnels: 80–90% open rate, 15–25% click-through, 15–25%
DM-to-sale for 10k–100k accounts) come almost entirely from Manychat's own
blog and Manychat-adjacent "creator success story" content, i.e. a
company selling DM-automation software citing its own best-case customers.
This is not neutral data — it is the marketing material of the vendor
whose product produces the number. Treat these as **the upper bound of a
best case**, not an expectation.

**What's actually well-supported**, independent of the vendor numbers:
- A DM conversation converts at a categorically higher rate than a cold
  link-in-bio, because it's a two-way exchange instead of a one-way click.
  This is just true of conversation vs. broadcast, doesn't need a specific
  citation.
- Smaller/more engaged audiences plausibly convert DMs better than huge
  ones, because the creator can plausibly be a real person replying, not
  a team running a script. This favors Nolan's current stage, not despite
  being small but *because* of it.

**Realistic planning number:** don't plan around 15-25% DM-to-sale. Plan
around **a small, real number of conversations per week becoming a small,
real number of calls**, and let logged data (via `/log-post` and
`/review`) replace the estimate as soon as there's real history. The
schema explicitly tracks "coaching conversations started" for exactly this
reason — so this file's guess gets replaced by Nolan's own numbers within
a few weeks.

## Free front door options

| Option | Setup cost | Fit for this account |
|---|---|---|
| Email capture (Google Form / ConvertKit / Beehiiv) | ~zero, 10 min | **Recommended for Phase 0** — matches blueprint §7, lowest effort, matches the "the follower is rented, the email is owned" principle from the teardown |
| Free Skool community | ~30 min setup, ongoing moderation | Defer — a community with no members yet is just an empty room; better introduced once there's an audience worth gathering (Phase 2 per teardown's build sequence) |
| Free webinar/training | Real prep time, recurring hosting | Defer — requires a "teach" posture Nolan hasn't earned yet per the credibility filter |

## Price ladder (from the teardown, unchanged — still the correct shape)

| Tier | Price | Purpose | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Capture + trust | Now |
| Low | $20–200 | Volume, buyer identification | Phase 2 (after coaching proof) |
| Mid | $300–1,000/mo | 1:1 coaching, real margin | Phase 1 — **start here, not at Free-tier product** |
| High | $3,000–10,000+ | Mentorship | Phase 4 only, once results exist |

The teardown's argument for starting at mid-tier instead of low-tier holds
up under the DM-conversion math above too: a handful of real conversations
converting into a handful of $300-800/mo clients is a more realistic near-
term outcome than needing hundreds of $47 buyers to hit the same revenue,
and it matches the "3-5 free/cheap clients now, sell 1:1 at month 3" phase
plan already given in the MMA build prompt.
