# Knowledge: Instagram Distribution Mechanics

*Compiled Aug 2026. This is a static reference — re-check items flagged below on
their stated interval, don't re-research the whole file every session.*

## Source quality warning — read this first

Instagram does not publish its ranking algorithm. Almost everything below
comes from one of three tiers, and they are not equally trustworthy:

1. **Primary** — statements directly attributed to Adam Mosseri (head of
   Instagram) in interviews/broadcasts he actually gave.
2. **Aggregated data** — vendor studies claiming to have analyzed real post
   samples (e.g. Buffer's "2 million posts"). Directionally useful, but the
   methodology isn't independently auditable and the numbers should be read
   as "roughly this shape," not as precise thresholds.
3. **SEO/marketing-blog folklore** — the vast majority of what ranks for
   "Instagram algorithm 2026" is content-marketing written to rank for that
   phrase, recycling the same claims from each other without a named source.
   Treat specific numbers from this tier (e.g. "DM shares are worth 3-5x
   likes," "10+ reposts in 30 days excludes you from non-follower reach")
   as **plausible but unverified** — directionally consistent with what
   Mosseri has said, but not something to build a strategy around trusting
   to the decimal.

Where a claim below is tier 1, it's marked **[confirmed]**. Tier 2 is
**[aggregated]**. Tier 3 is **[unverified]**.

## Ranking signals (re-check: every 6 months — platform changes this often)

- **Watch time / completion rate** is the top-weighted signal for Reels
  ranking. **[confirmed, Mosseri, Jan 2026]** — this is the single most
  load-bearing fact for the whole content system: the hook's job is to
  produce watch time, not likes.
- **Sends per reach (DM shares)** and **likes per reach** are the next
  tier of confirmed signals. **[confirmed]** A viewer sending your reel to
  a friend is a stronger signal than a like — it says "this is worth
  someone else's attention," which is a harder bar than a passive tap.
- Comments and saves also factor in but are weighted below
  watch-time/shares. **[aggregated]**
- Numeric multipliers like "DM shares worth 3-5x likes" are **[unverified]**
  — real direction (shares > likes), unverifiable exact weight.

## The "audition" / non-follower reach pool (re-check: 6 months)

- New reels are shown to a small sample of non-followers first (a "trial"
  pool). If early watch-time/engagement from that sample is strong, reach
  expands outward — first to more non-followers, then to followers.
  **[aggregated, consistent with Mosseri's public description of
  Explore/Reels distribution]**
- Practical implication: **the first ~30 minutes to a few hours of a
  reel's life matters disproportionately.** If you're going to reply to
  comments (§ time budget priority #1), doing it on a reel in its first
  hour is worth more than the same 10 minutes spent later — it's feeding
  the signal while the audition is still running.
- Follower count is not itself a ranking input for reach on a given reel —
  a 200-follower account's reel and a 200k-follower account's reel enter
  the same trial pool on the same terms. **[aggregated]** This is the
  single most important fact for a near-zero account: **you are not
  reach-throttled for being small.** You are throttled for weak early
  retention, same as anyone else.

## Posting frequency (re-check: 6 months)

- **[aggregated, Buffer]** Reach/follower-growth show diminishing returns
  above ~5 posts/week; 3–5/week roughly doubles follower growth vs. 1–2/wk.
  1 post/day is claimed to capture ~80% of max potential reach.
- **[unverified]** Specific "3+/day triggers a 30% unfollow spike" figures
  — plausible shape (over-posting fatigues an audience), not a number to
  trust precisely.
- **[confirmed, Mosseri]** Instagram's own stated position is that posting
  more grows reach, because more content means more discovery
  opportunities and more shareable moments — but this is Instagram's
  incentive talking as much as neutral advice.
- **Read for this account:** the blueprint's 1 reel/day is within the
  well-supported "no clear penalty" range (well under the ~5/wk-diminishing
  point isn't quite right — 1/day is 7/wk — see conflict note below).

**Conflict flag:** the blueprint (30 reels in 30 days = 7/week) sits
*above* the range where aggregated data says returns diminish (~5/week).
The MMA time-budget already overrides this for a different reason (the
30-min floor can't sustainably produce a daily reel) — but worth naming
explicitly: **daily posting is not obviously optimal per the research
itself**, independent of Nolan's time constraint. 4–5 quality reels/week
is better supported by the evidence than 7.

## What causes suppression (re-check: 6 months)

- **[confirmed]** Reposted content carrying another platform's watermark
  (TikTok, CapCut export watermarks) is suppressed from Explore/Reels
  recommendations. Always export/film native, no watermarks.
- **[unverified]** A hard "10+ reposts in 30 days = zero non-follower
  reach" rule — directionally real (Instagram penalizes recycled/aggregator
  content) but the specific threshold is unconfirmed. Not relevant here
  since this account posts original footage.
- Early-account behavior (first ~10-20 posts) disproportionately shapes
  what audience Instagram thinks you're for — **[aggregated]**, and
  consistent with the blueprint's "test all four pillars in week 1, then
  double down" approach: it's not just a discovery exercise for Nolan,
  it's also training the algorithm's model of the account early, when that
  model is cheapest to shape.

## Bottom line for this account

1. Hook exists to buy watch time, full stop — that's the top confirmed
   signal.
2. Replying to comments in a reel's first hour is not just "engagement
   busywork" — it's timed to the audition window. This upgrades comment
   engagement (already ceiling priority #1) to something worth doing fast,
   not just eventually.
3. Small-account status is not a reach penalty. The ceiling is retention
   quality, not follower count.
4. 4–5 reels/week is better evidenced than 7/week — this account's
   30-day slate (`content/calendar.md`) is already built at ~5/week for
   that reason. If even that isn't holding under the floor-day rule
   (§ CLAUDE.md), cutting further is supported by the research, not just
   a fallback.
